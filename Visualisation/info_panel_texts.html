      Overview

      The Metabolic Reference Atlas covers [[n_pairs]] organ-organ pairs across [[n_organs]] organs: adrenal glands, bone marrow, brain, colon, heart, kidney, liver, lung, muscle, pancreas, small intestine, spleen, thyroid, and white adipose tissue (WAT).

      The atlas covers seven disease-conditions with All papers combining them. The disease-conditions were assigned by an LLM based on the abstract and title of each paper. Each shown organ-organ connection has at least found paper. In addition, partial-correlation networks based on FDG-PET are available for three cohorts (mean ± SD): healthy (BMI ≤ 25 kg/m², n = 112; BMI 22.3 ± 1.9 kg/m², age 33.8 ± 12.6 years), overweight (BMI > 25 kg/m², n = 85; BMI 30.6 ± 3.4 kg/m², age 34.4 ± 10.9 years) and lung cancer (n = 241; BMI 25.4 ± 4.6 kg/m², age 64.9 ± 10.3 years). Age is known for 99, 77 and 240 of these patients. Partial correlation networks are based on the work of Geist et al. (2025) Edge colour shows the sign of the partial correlation (blue positive, red negative).

      The pipeline of the atlas uses a PubMed query requiring both organs, at least one metabolic keyword and at least one crosstalk keyword in the title or abstract. After fetching, papers are filtered further: kept are papers where both organs names and a crosstalk keyword appear together in the same sentence. In addition, a multi-organ rule is applied to select only actual relatopnships.

      Duplicate Papers

      Each paper is fetched independently for every organ pair whose PubMed query it matches, so the same PMID can appear in multiple pairs (e.g., a paper on liver-gut-brain crosstalk may pass the same-sentence filter for both liver-small intestine and liver-brain). The condition filter gives every paper a condition label.

      Search Query (regex-based)

      Each organ contributes its MeSH term and all text aliases searched in Title/Abstract. Both organ clauses must co-occur with at least one metabolic keyword and at least one crosstalk keyword:

      (MeSH_A OR aliases_A[Title/Abstract]) AND (MeSH_B OR aliases_B[Title/Abstract]) AND ([[kw_block]]) AND ([[crosstalk_kw_block]])

      Organ and crosstalk terms are matched as whole words not substrings so e.g. "renal" does not match to "adrenal". A match is also discarded if it's negated, e.g. "non-renal", "nonrenal", "non renal" or "not renal" don't count as a positive mention of that organ or crosstalk term.

      Same-Sentence Filter (regex-based)

      A paper is kept only if there's a single sentence in its title/abstract containing organ A's name/alias, organ B's name/alias, and a crosstalk keyword all together.

      A multi-organ rule also guards against sentences that name a third organ: e.g. "the pancreas modulates protein expression in the heart and colon" describes two pancreas relationships, not a heart-colon one. Organ mentions joined only by list words (",", "and", "or", "/", "&", "as well as") form one group. A pair is rejected if both organs are in the same group, if they are parallel sites joined by a repeated location word ("in the brain and in the lung"), or if another organ outside their groups is named before the later of the two organs. Organs named only after both are ignored.

      Condition Filter (LLM-based)

      Every paper that passed the same-sentence filter is classified by an LLM ([[cond_llm_model]], via [[cond_llm_backend]]). It reads the title/abstract and assigns the paper to one of seven predefined disease-conditions.

      [[cond_label_rows]]
      Label Meaning Papers

      To account for LLM errors, the LLM must also return the evidence sentence that led to its decision. A regex tests if the evidence sentence (at least four words) occurs in the title or abstract in the same order. If not, the answer counts as a hallucination and the paper is not used for any condition view.

      [[cond_filter_stats]]
      Key Player Extraction

      The title, abstract and MeSH descriptors of the papers of a pair are scanned against three vocabularies. A term and its synonyms count once per paper:

      • Hormones ([[n_hormones]] terms) — insulin, glucagon, cortisol, leptin, GLP-1, IGF-1, FGF21, irisin, …
      • Metabolites ([[n_metabolites]] terms) — based on the Stanford Pathways of Human Metabolism map: glucose, lactate, fatty acids, bile acids, amino acids, ATP, …
      • Proteins ([[n_proteins]] terms) — all Human Protein Atlas proteins with evidence at protein level (gene name, symbol and hyphenated variants such as "IL2" / "IL-2"); protein families are merged (e.g. AKT1-3, GLUT1-5)

      A hormone that also appears in another list is counted only as a hormone. Terms identical to one of the 10,000 most frequent English words were removed (e.g. "was" for WASP actin nucleation promoting factor).

      Otsuka-Ochiai Coefficient

      For each organ pair (A, B) the Otsuka-Ochiai (OO) coefficient normalises the raw co-occurrence paper count by the geometric mean of each organ's total paper pool, correcting for organs with a large literature base that would otherwise dominate any raw co-mention count.

      ## The formula is typeset with plain HTML/CSS (fraction bar = border of .oo-den, square root = sign + overline), no external library.
      OO(A, B) = npapers(A ↔ B) √npapers(A) × npapers(B)

      npapers(A) is the union of unique PubMed IDs across all pairs involving organ A in that view (the same same-sentence-filtered paper set used throughout). The coefficient values are between [0, 1].

      Threshold Selection (Kneedle / Elbow)

      The non-zero OO values of all pairs of a view are sorted in ascending order and the kneedle algorithm (rolling-mean smoothing → log-transform → normalise → maximise diagonal distance) identifies the "elbow", so the point of maximum curvature where adding more edges yields diminishing evidence quality. Pairs with OO ≥ elbow are considered evidence-supported connections.

      Charts on the right show the OO distribution (bars, sorted descending) and the elbow threshold (red dashed line) for each condition. Coloured bars are above the threshold and grey bars fall below the threshold. In the network visualization, only showing edges above the threshold can be selected.
